# Community operations and moderation

Reviewed: 2026-08-29

A community channel is a live service with people, sensitive reports and predictable abuse pressure. Define rules, staffing and escalation before opening it; do not make unpaid volunteers the only safety layer.

## Operating model

Publish concise rules with examples, scope and enforcement ladder. Define moderator roles, least-privilege permissions, coverage, training, confidentiality, evidence retention, conflicts of interest, wellbeing/rotation and an authority escalation path.

Provide report, block/mute, appeal and emergency channels. Prioritize imminent safety, child exploitation, credible threats, doxxing and account compromise according to platform/legal escalation requirements. Do not investigate serious real-world harm publicly.

## Enforcement record

Store case ID, policy/version, timestamps, evidence pointers, reporter safety class, action, duration, moderator, rationale, communication and appeal outcome. Restrict access and retention; moderation evidence often contains the most sensitive community data.

Use automation for triage, spam/rate limits and known patterns, not as unexplained final authority for ambiguous cases. Measure report response, repeat harm, appeal reversals and moderator load—not raw ban count.

## Developer communication

Separate announcements, support, bug reports and social discussion. Acknowledge confirmed incidents, avoid promising dates without confidence, publish accessible patch notes and close the loop on known issues. Do not argue with every review or remove criticism merely because it is negative.

Steam provides reported-post queues, moderator roles and official developer responses. Discord combines user reports, proactive detection, automated tools and human investigation; a game community still needs its own narrower rules and staff process.
